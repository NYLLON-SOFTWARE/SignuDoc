# FDA certification letter (11.100(c))

Before or at the time it starts using electronic signatures, an organization certifies to the FDA that its electronic signatures are meant to be the legally binding equivalent of handwritten ones. 21 CFR 11.100(c) says the letter is submitted on paper and signed by hand. It names the office to send it to, and that office has been reorganized since the rule was written, so check the FDA's current instructions for where and how to send it before you do. The letter is sent once for the organization, not once per system.

---

`[Organization letterhead]`

`[Date]`

`[FDA office and address, per current FDA instructions]`

**Subject: Certification of electronic signatures under 21 CFR 11.100(c)**

Pursuant to Section 11.100 of Title 21 of the Code of Federal Regulations, this is to certify that `[organization's legal name]` intends that all electronic signatures executed by our employees, agents, or representatives, located anywhere in the world, are the legally binding equivalent of traditional handwritten signatures.

`[Optional: FDA establishment identifiers, such as FEI numbers, or a list of sites covered.]`

Signed,

`[Handwritten signature]`

`[Name]`
`[Title, for example Head of Quality]`
`[Organization]`
`[Address]`
`[Telephone and email]`

---

Keep a copy with your validation records. Under 11.100(c)(2), the FDA can also ask for further certification or testimony that a specific electronic signature is the legally binding equivalent of the signer's handwritten signature.
